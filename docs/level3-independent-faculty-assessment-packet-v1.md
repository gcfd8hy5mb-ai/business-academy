# Business Academy Level 3 — independent faculty evaluation packet (Version 1)
**Date:** 2026-11-01. **Status:** REVIEWER-READY RUBRICS ONLY — NO INDEPENDENT REVIEWS HAVE BEEN PERFORMED.

## Purpose and limitations
This packet creates explicit instructions for qualified external evaluators of Level 3, courses 14–20. It does not claim accreditation, school affiliation, college credit, a university MBA degree, or academic equivalence. The app's local self-scored rubrics are formative and must not be copied into an independent grade. Existing hypothetical cases may be used for practice; qualifying submissions require unfamiliar questions or new data and documented independent work.

## Common evidence package supplied to a reviewer
Each submission includes: (1) anonymized student ID; (2) assessment ID and version; (3) as-of decision date and scenario/data provenance; (4) source register with links, reporting periods, exact locations and claim mapping; (5) editable/reproducible model with documented input assumptions and units; (6) original ex-ante executive recommendation; (7) downside and counterfactual; (8) actual outcome or ex-post analysis only when applicable, clearly separated; (9) student declaration of assistance and source access; (10) evaluator feedback and resubmission history.

**Method:** Two qualified, conflict-free reviewers independently mark at least two substantial artifacts per course without seeing the learner's self-ratings. Scores should be compared for disagreement; unresolved discrepancies go to a third reviewer. At least one unseen integrated final defense must be evaluated separately. This is a proposed internal standard, not an accreditor's required procedure.

## Consistent analytic scoring rubric (each artifact, 100 points)
| Category | Points | High-quality observable evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Mathematical and technical accuracy | 30 | Reproducible calculations, timing and units; independently checked constraints, no concealed assumptions |
| Executive reasoning and alternatives | 25 | Counterfactual, at least two feasible strategies, downside sensitivity and decision thresholds |
| Source evidence and research | 20 | Primary dated sources, transparent extraction, source quality, uncertainty and citation completeness |
| Risk, governance and ethics | 15 | Legal boundaries, stakeholders, control owners, mitigation and stop/appeal criteria |
| Executive communication | 10 | Concise structured board argument, clear model exhibits, defensible written/oral responses |

**Proposed project acceptance gate:** 80/100 or above by reconciled independent marks; no critical failure in the course-specific category below; reviewer documents rationale. A critical flaw means material result unreliable or serious legal/ethical oversight. Resubmit independently after targeted remediation. Student self-ratings do not satisfy this gate.

## Seven reviewer assignment briefs
### Course 14 — Corporate Finance & Valuation

**Required unfamiliar assessment:** Five-year audited-source FCFF/DCF valuation and alternative acquisition decision

**Reviewer checks:** Reconcile EBIT to after-tax operating cash flow and balance-sheet reinvestment; estimate a currency-consistent cost of capital; create terminal value and EV-to-equity bridge; evaluate leverage covenant and downside liquidity.

**Critical failure examples:** A mis-signed ΔNWC, unsourced WACC, double-counted financing tax shield, or false claim about the company filing.

**Required evidence:** One original model workbook/code notebook, a 1,500–2,500 word sourced board memorandum, one documented adversarial response and a 20–30 minute live or recorded defense. Reviewer must log which primary documents and calculations were actually checked.

### Course 15 — Competitive Strategy

**Required unfamiliar assessment:** Competitive-defense recommendation based on dated segment reporting and two named rivals

**Reviewer checks:** Map forces, activities, differentiation and competitive moves; build quantitative contribution, market share and rival response; test cannibalization; explain which evidence would falsify the strategy.

**Critical failure examples:** Revenue growth equated with contribution growth, unsourced causal attribution, or unlawful coordination proposal.

**Required evidence:** One original model workbook/code notebook, a 1,500–2,500 word sourced board memorandum, one documented adversarial response and a 20–30 minute live or recorded defense. Reviewer must log which primary documents and calculations were actually checked.

### Course 16 — Managerial Economics

**Required unfamiliar assessment:** Empirical price/capacity/procurement choice with explicit constrained optimization

**Reviewer checks:** Derive demand sensitivity and marginal contribution; solve integer capacity allocations and shadow values; distinguish opportunity from accounting costs; test regulatory and model limits.

**Critical failure examples:** Unjustified price elasticity, infeasible production allocation, or omission of binding opportunity cost.

**Required evidence:** One original model workbook/code notebook, a 1,500–2,500 word sourced board memorandum, one documented adversarial response and a 20–30 minute live or recorded defense. Reviewer must log which primary documents and calculations were actually checked.

### Course 17 — Business Analytics & Statistics

**Required unfamiliar assessment:** Reproducible randomized or quasi-experimental operating decision

**Reviewer checks:** Include a raw or clearly synthetic labeled dataset, prespecified primary outcome, confidence intervals, missingness, assignment balance, forecast benchmark, sensitivity and counterfactual.

**Critical failure examples:** Misinterpreting a non-significant result as proof, data leakage, wrong denominator, undisclosed post-hoc slicing, or missing reproducible calculations.

**Required evidence:** One original model workbook/code notebook, a 1,500–2,500 word sourced board memorandum, one documented adversarial response and a 20–30 minute live or recorded defense. Reviewer must log which primary documents and calculations were actually checked.

### Course 18 — Entrepreneurship & Venture Capital

**Required unfamiliar assessment:** Multi-round cap table, liquidation waterfall, runway and financing fallback

**Reviewer checks:** Model shares on consistent pre/post-money denominators, convertible rights and option pool; compare financing outcomes across exit distributions and cash timing.

**Critical failure examples:** Preference treated as an additive entitlement when nonparticipating, incorrect dilution denominator, or assuming financing arrives without contingency.

**Required evidence:** One original model workbook/code notebook, a 1,500–2,500 word sourced board memorandum, one documented adversarial response and a 20–30 minute live or recorded defense. Reviewer must log which primary documents and calculations were actually checked.

### Course 19 — International Business

**Required unfamiliar assessment:** Cross-border capital budget with treasury hedge and jurisdiction risks

**Reviewer checks:** Use matched local-currency cash flows and discount rates, forward/spot settlements, country restrictions, repatriation, sourcing resilience and global integration.

**Critical failure examples:** Mixing currencies without conversion or FX-consistent rates, asserting speculative hedge profits are guaranteed, or invented regulatory facts.

**Required evidence:** One original model workbook/code notebook, a 1,500–2,500 word sourced board memorandum, one documented adversarial response and a 20–30 minute live or recorded defense. Reviewer must log which primary documents and calculations were actually checked.

### Course 20 — Technology, AI & Innovation

**Required unfamiliar assessment:** AI adoption board decision with quantified error cost and independent oversight

**Reviewer checks:** Build baseline versus proposed operating economics, subgroup test outcomes, escalation and appeals, security/data governance, adverse incident stress and thresholds to stop deployment.

**Critical failure examples:** Treating raw accuracy as fairness, withholding material error risk, claiming human oversight from nonexistent review, or ignoring safety/legal constraints.

**Required evidence:** One original model workbook/code notebook, a 1,500–2,500 word sourced board memorandum, one documented adversarial response and a 20–30 minute live or recorded defense. Reviewer must log which primary documents and calculations were actually checked.

## External evaluator qualifications and governance
Finance and accounting reviewers should have advanced financial analysis and valuation teaching/practice experience; strategy/economics/analytics reviewers must be technically competent to reproduce the relevant models; venture, international and AI reviewers must have the corresponding specialist knowledge. Record degree(s), teaching and industry experience, conflict declaration, compensation relationship, reviewer scope, date and assessment materials. An external reviewer cannot certify an entire degree by inspecting a single course.

## Review completion checklist
The internal desk review is only a planning stage. Do not mark independent academic sign-off complete until qualified evaluator identities, inspected source/data records, independent scores, learner artifacts, reviewer disagreement handling, remedial outcomes and a signed dated conclusion are attached. Claims about comparability must state precisely *which* competencies and artifacts were externally assessed, rather than calling Level 3 equivalent to a whole MBA degree.
