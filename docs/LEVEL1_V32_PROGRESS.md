# Level 1 development on the approved Version 31 interface

The Version 31 main branch was independently measured before editing: the six Level 1 authored sequences contained 111.50 estimated assigned hours, leaving 253.50 hours against the original 365-hour plan. ## Batch 1 — Business Mathematics: capital-budget model

This original fictional Meridian case adds two connected lessons (90 minutes) on dated unlevered free cash flows and sensitivity/break-even analysis; two 60-minute written applications with worked solutions and rubrics; a 120-minute closed-note objective and written section exam (five objective items, an 800–1,000 word board analysis, answer explanations and targeted lesson remediation); and a 120-minute public-corporation capital-allocation portfolio. The **7.50 hours** are explicit study assignments, not a claim of measured student effort. The course grows from 18.50 to 26.00 hours. Level 1 grows to **119.00 / 365.00 hours**, leaving **246.00 hours**; the program grows to **145.75 hours**.

The model requires equipment and working capital at t=0, after-tax operating cash for five years, taxed salvage and NWC recovery at t=5, downside and break-even tests, and a perfect-information upper bound for a pilot. The portfolio demands dated corporate filings and distinguishes observed disclosures from hypothetical project inputs. Actual company figures are not invented. The material builds on the publicly available [MIT Sloan Finance Theory I syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-401-finance-theory-i-fall-2008/pages/syllabus/) and [problem sets](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-401-finance-theory-i-fall-2008/pages/problem-sets/) as a scope benchmark; none of their questions or solutions were reproduced.

Fresh IDs are used for the two lessons, applications, exam and portfolio. The shared learning model accepts additional section exams and portfolio studies without another navigation system. Section mastery requires a new objective score of at least 80%, a separately self-reviewed written analysis, and a separately self-reviewed portfolio. Earlier answer snapshots, drafts and local storage remain intact. The professor uses four authored reasoning steps per lesson; it cannot evaluate free-form writing. Objective feedback includes the full calculation, specific remediation and a jump to the relevant lesson.
