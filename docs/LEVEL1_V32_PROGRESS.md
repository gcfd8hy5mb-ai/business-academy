# Level 1 development on the approved Version 31 interface

The Version 31 main branch was independently measured before editing: the six Level 1 authored sequences contained 111.50 estimated assigned hours, leaving 253.50 hours against the original 365-hour plan. The entire program contained 138.25 assigned hours. Version 31's focused one-lesson-at-a-time view and prior assessments are retained.

## Batch 1 — Business Mathematics: capital-budget model

This original fictional Meridian case adds two connected lessons (90 minutes) on dated unlevered free cash flows and sensitivity/break-even analysis; two 60-minute written applications with worked solutions and rubrics; a 120-minute closed-note objective and written section exam (five objective items, an 800–1,000 word board analysis, answer explanations and targeted lesson remediation); and a 120-minute public-corporation capital-allocation portfolio. The **7.50 hours** are explicit study assignments, not a claim of measured student effort. The course grows from 18.50 to 26.00 hours. Level 1 grows to **119.00 / 365.00 hours**, leaving **246.00 hours**; the program grows to **145.75 hours**.

The model requires equipment and working capital at t=0, after-tax operating cash for five years, taxed salvage and NWC recovery at t=5, downside and break-even tests, and a perfect-information upper bound for a pilot. The portfolio demands dated corporate filings and distinguishes observed disclosures from hypothetical project inputs. Actual company figures are not invented. The material builds on the publicly available [MIT Sloan Finance Theory I syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-401-finance-theory-i-fall-2008/pages/syllabus/) and [problem sets](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-401-finance-theory-i-fall-2008/pages/problem-sets/) as a scope benchmark; none of their questions or solutions were reproduced.

Fresh IDs are used for the two lessons, applications, exam and portfolio. The shared learning model accepts additional section exams and portfolio studies without another navigation system. Section mastery requires a new objective score of at least 80%, a separately self-reviewed written analysis, and a separately self-reviewed portfolio. Earlier answer snapshots, drafts and local storage remain intact. The professor uses four authored reasoning steps per lesson; it cannot evaluate free-form writing. Objective feedback includes the full calculation, specific remediation and a jump to the relevant lesson.

## Remaining academic and quality work

All six subjects still fall short of their planned full workloads. Business Mathematics needs more algebra, statistics, probability, modeling, data interpretation and cumulative timed exams. Other Level 1 subjects need more instruction and independently validated assessment. The current written rubrics are self-review, not instructor grading or secure proctoring. Real iPhone Safari and Home Screen keyboard behavior still require device testing. No university credit or academic equivalence is claimed.

## Batch 2 — Financial Literacy: cash quality and credit stress

Two further focused lessons (90 minutes) teach the indirect CFO bridge, capex-aware cash measurement and contract-defined covenant tests. Two 60-minute written problems require reconciliation of an acquisition-related receivable movement and a combined earnings/debt/rate shock. A new 120-minute examination contains five objective calculations with explanations, lesson-linked remediation and an 800–1,000 word liquidity memo. A 120-minute portfolio dossier requires a real public-company annual report, debt note and maturity map, with a separately labeled fictional stress. The case data are fictional; the portfolio must cite actual disclosures and identify missing definitions rather than fabricate them.

This is **7.50 more assigned hours**. Financial Literacy rises from 18.50 to 26.00 hours; the cumulative two-batch Level 1 total is **126.50 / 365.00 hours**, leaving **238.50 hours**. The program total is **153.25 hours**. The [HBS Online financial statement analysis guide](https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/financial-statement-analysis) is a public supplemental scope reference, not an HBS course or copied assignment.

Batch 2 has distinct lesson, written-problem, exam and portfolio IDs. Existing financial-literacy submissions and the earlier advanced case remain available in the Version 31 lesson flow. The new exam and portfolio are required for the expanded self-assessed sequence; no automated judgment of the written quality is claimed.
