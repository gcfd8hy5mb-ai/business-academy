## Batch 1 — Business Mathematics: inference, probability, regression and allocation

Two new modules add four lessons (180 minutes) with worked examples and staged tutoring: sampling and t inference; discrete payoffs and Bayes decision value; least-squares regression and causal limits; and a binary capital-allocation program with two binding resource constraints. Four independent written problems (240 minutes) include reproducible solutions and limitations. Two five-question objective plus written exams (240 minutes) each require a separate 800–1,000 word analysis; objective responses receive answer explanations and lesson-linked remediation. Two public-company quantitative notebooks (240 minutes) require source citations and explicitly fictional decisions. **15.00 estimated assigned hours** are added, bringing Business Mathematics to **41.00 hours** and Level 1 to **171.50 / 365 hours**, with **193.50 hours** still planned.

The statistical inference assumptions are deliberately explicit: a five-observation t procedure cannot establish a population fact if divisions are selected nonrandomly or share shocks. The regression example has a wide slope interval despite R²=.70; association is not asserted to be causal. The portfolio allocation model treats projects as indivisible; optimization is conditional on additive NPV and correlated downside checks. All cases and solutions are original fictional work. Scope references are [MIT OCW 18.05 probability and statistics](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/) and its [problem sets](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/pages/problem-sets/), the [Wharton MBA statistics descriptions](https://statistics.wharton.upenn.edu/programs/mba/course-descriptions/) and the [HBS Online regression overview](https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/what-is-regression-analysis). They are linked references, not copied assignments or claims of affiliation.

The two new module, exam, written, portfolio and problem IDs are distinct from all earlier evidence. Existing local storage and scores are retained. Both new objective exams require at least 80% and both written cases and portfolios require explicit self-review at 3/4 on every criterion; no essay is automatically judged.
