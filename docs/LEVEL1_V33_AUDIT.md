## Batch 1 — Course 01: enterprise design and capital allocation

Two new lessons (90 minutes) cover matrix decision rights and a reconciled segment-to-group after-tax capital budget. Two written 60-minute problems provide full worked solutions on forecast overlap, governance, corporate overhead, economic profit and a joint downside. A new 120-minute five-item objective plus written examination gives explicit calculations, remediation links and an 800–1,000 word board case. A 120-minute public-company portfolio requires a dated filing, segment reconciliation, fictional investment alternatives and a decision-rights map. **7.50 assigned hours** are added; Course 01 reaches 26.00 hours and Level 1 reaches **134.00 / 365 hours**, leaving **231.00 hours**. Program total is **160.75 hours**.

This original case uses fictional Helios figures. The public [Harvard Business School Working Knowledge discussion of strategy alignment](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/managing-alignment-as-a-process) and the prior [Wharton faculty accounting discussion](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/finance-accounting-for-the-rest-of-us-a-conversation-with-richard-a-lambert/) inform scope and source critique, not the case text or endorsement.

Exam and portfolio evidence have new `c01-v33` IDs, so earlier submissions remain available. The local professor reveals four authored reasoning stages for each lesson. The new objective exam requires 80% and its written case and portfolio require explicit self-review. This does not constitute independent grading.

## Batch 2 — Course 03: contract cohorts and cash conversion

Two new lessons (90 minutes) reconcile full-year contract cohorts, retained dollars, direct contribution, acquisition spending, operating profit and receivables. Two 60-minute written problems have complete calculations for a dashboard audit and correlated renewal/acquisition/collection stress. A 120-minute five-question and written examination includes calculation explanations, targeted lesson remediation and an 800–1,000 word board forecast. A 120-minute portfolio requires a dated public-company revenue-recognition note and an explicitly fictional customer model. Its assessments use distinct `c03-v33` IDs.

This adds **7.50 assigned hours**. Course 03 reaches **26.00 hours**; cumulative Level 1 is **141.50 / 365.00 hours**, leaving **223.50 hours**; program total is **168.25 hours**. The fictional Orion assumptions place all contracts at the year start for full-year service. The lesson explicitly explains why staggered service and cash collection need separate schedules. The public [MIT Sloan Economic Analysis for Business Decisions](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-010-economic-analysis-for-business-decisions-fall-2004/) is a benchmark for pricing, cost and decision analysis, not a copied or credited Business Academy course.

## Batch 3 — Course 04: subsidiary financing and runway

Two lessons (90 minutes) compare an internal venture, controlled subsidiary and external financing through entity/control questions, primary versus secondary equity, debt obligations and a cash-floor timeline. Two written 60-minute problems include complete cap-table and base/downside runway solutions. A 120-minute five-item plus written exam includes lesson-linked remediation and a board financing case; a 120-minute portfolio calls for an actual public filing, hypothetical venture terms and a monthly cash schedule. The cases identify jurisdiction-specific formation and consolidation as matters for professional review. They do not present a particular legal structure as universally appropriate.

The additional **7.50 hours** take Course 04 to **26.00 hours**, Level 1 to **149.00 / 365.00 hours**, with **216.00 hours remaining**, and the program to **175.75 hours**. The original financing case is distinct from the earlier single-round dilution and liquidation-preference lessons. [Stanford STVP's customer discovery material](https://stvp.stanford.edu/av/acting-customer-discovery) remains a public supplemental benchmark for stage evidence; no university case text was copied.

## Batch 4 — Course 05: macro scenario and cross-border response

Two lessons (90 minutes) connect monetary policy, floating interest, inflation, trade tariff and currency translation in a single fictional multinational bridge. Two 60-minute written problems give full solutions for base/downside earnings and a partial natural-currency hedge versus risky price pass-through. A 120-minute five-question and written board exam has calculation explanations and lesson-linked remediation. A 120-minute public-company portfolio requires dated geographic, debt and risk disclosures, then a separately labeled fictional macro scenario. The case avoids presenting a central-bank announcement as a reliable company forecast.

The additional **7.50 hours** take Course 05 to **26.50 hours**, Level 1 to **156.50 / 365.00 hours**, leaving **208.50 hours**, and the program to **183.25 hours**. The public [Yale game theory course](https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-159) is a benchmark for strategic responses and the existing [Oxford Saïd board discussion](https://www.sbs.ox.ac.uk/oxford-answers/five-questions-boards-should-ask-about-purpose) supports board evidence critique. Neither source is an endorsement or copied teaching text.
